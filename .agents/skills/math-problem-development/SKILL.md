name: maths-problem-development
description: Create and use high quality, appropriately challenging maths questions for students, with meaningful hints and mark feedback.
---

Adapt approach according to the topic in focus and the qualification specification and example question papers.

Questions must be carefully and accurately pitched the required challenge level. A bad example of this would be 4324 / 4 for a 3rd column division question. The numerator may be larger, but 4 is an easily found factor, which avoid the need to use the bus stop method. A more appropriate demoninator is this case would be 5 or 7. Another bad example would be 3021 x 246, because most of the digit products are < 10 and the their will be no carry digits. A better example would be 67529 x 837, or 9483 x 8976. The 2nd and 3rd column needs need to test the ability of students to use the method.

Question hints must be specific to the question, and slightly but not completely reduce the challenge level of a question. This could be a hint at the first step, a reminder about a concept that could help in the specific case of this question, or question that jogs the learner to think about a possible approach to the solution. Hints could naturally be quite similar for similar problems, but care should always be taken to ensure the hint is a genuine and specific hint for the problem it is given for. 

The display of hints should be made around the area of the 'hint' button, when it is pressed, and the hint hidden when the button is pressed again.

As with hints, any automarking feedback or correct answer display notes should also be specific to the question and the most likely mistake(s).
