# M04 — Ratio: sharing and missing amounts

**Status: Draft — awaiting review. Not approved for PowerPoint.**

This is the core ratio set. The user has also requested ratio problems; a separate application set is planned in [TOPIC_PLAN.md](TOPIC_PLAN.md), including recipes, mixtures, scale, costs and changed quantities. This draft alone does not complete the ratio topic.

## Scope and delivery

Refresh ratio problem solving. Students distinguish a total, one known amount and a difference, find the value of one part, then answer the actual question. This is one seven-slide module, not a lesson introducing ratio notation.

Use the established columns **Thread 1: Guided**, **Thread 2: Core**, **Thread 3: Depth**. Guided shares a total; Core starts with one known amount; Depth starts with a difference. These are different reasoning demands, not a requirement that every ratio question have only one or two arithmetic operations. Keep the column headings neutral on student slides: do not add “divide by total parts” or similar hints.

No calculator needed for these chosen values. Students record working in booklets or a webapp. All ratios refer to quantities in the order named. Answers to practice questions belong in notes and the teacher key, not on student slides.

The independent slides vary ratios, which amount is known, which amount is larger, and whether students must find one amount or the total. Do not teach “always add the ratio numbers” or “always divide by the first ratio number”.

## Slide 1 — Worked demo

Title: **Ratio: sharing and missing amounts**

Instruction: **Follow each step down your column.**

| Row | Thread 1: Guided | Thread 2: Core | Thread 3: Depth |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The Full Question | A and B share £120 in the ratio 2:3. How much does B receive? | Red and blue paint are mixed in the ratio 3:5. There are 18 litres of red paint. How many litres of blue paint are needed? | Red and blue counters are in the ratio 3:7. There are 28 more blue counters than red counters. How many counters are there altogether? |
| Keywords & Calculation | “share £120”: the total is known. 2 + 3 = 5 parts. £120 ÷ 5, then × 3. | “18 litres of red”: 3 parts are known. 18 ÷ 3, then × 5. | “28 more blue”: the difference is known. 7 − 3 = 4 parts. 28 ÷ 4, then × (3 + 7). |
| Written Method | £120 ÷ 5 = £24 per part. £24 × 3 = £72. | 18 ÷ 3 = 6 litres per part. 6 × 5 = 30 litres. | 28 ÷ 4 = 7 counters per part. 3 + 7 = 10 parts. 7 × 10 = 70 counters. |
| Ballpark Check & Math | B gets more than half of £120, so more than £60. £72 fits. Check both shares: £48 + £72 = £120. | Blue is between one and two times the red amount, so between 18 and 36 litres. 30 litres fits. | The total represents 10 parts and the difference 4. The total should be between 2 and 3 times 28: between 56 and 84. 70 fits. |
| Final Answer | B receives £72. | 30 litres of blue paint. | 70 counters altogether. |

### Diagram specification

Use editable, equal-sized boxes. Align the two bars at the left; each box represents one part, not one pound, litre or counter. Label both bars by name; colour is optional, not the only way to identify them.

- Guided: A has 2 boxes; B has 3. A bracket covering both bars is labelled “£120 altogether”. Show “1 part = £24”.
- Core: red has 3 boxes labelled “18 litres”; blue has 5. Show “1 part = 6 litres”.
- Depth: red has 3 boxes; blue has 7. Bracket the **four extra blue boxes** and label them “28 more”. Show “1 part = 7 counters”. Do not label the overlap as the difference.

Keep the complete question and all four steps on this slide. Fit the diagrams beside the concise working within the Written Method row. Do not introduce a separate step-per-slide sequence.

## Slide 2 — Step-by-step practice 1

Instruction: **Complete each step in your booklet or webapp.** Use the four practice prompts above.

| Thread 1: Guided | Thread 2: Core | Thread 3: Depth |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A and B share £84 in the ratio 3:4. How much does A receive? | Notebooks and folders are in the ratio 4:7. There are 35 folders. How many notebooks are there? | Adults and children are in the ratio 2:5. There are 24 more children than adults. How many people are there altogether? |

Teacher working:

- T1: 3 + 4 = 7; £84 ÷ 7 = £12; £12 × 3 = **£36**. Less than half of £84; other share £48.
- T2: 35 ÷ 7 = 5; 5 × 4 = **20 notebooks**. Fewer notebooks than folders.
- T3: 5 − 2 = 3; 24 ÷ 3 = 8; (2 + 5) × 8 = **56 people**. 16 adults and 40 children; difference 24.

## Slide 3 — Step-by-step practice 2

Instruction and row prompts match Slide 2 exactly.

| Thread 1: Guided | Thread 2: Core | Thread 3: Depth |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A and B share £96 in the ratio 5:7. How much does B receive? | Juice and water are mixed in the ratio 2:7. There are 18 litres of juice. How many litres of water are needed? | Two lengths of ribbon, A and B, are in the ratio 5:8. B is 21 cm longer than A. What is their total length? |

Teacher working:

- T1: 5 + 7 = 12; £96 ÷ 12 = £8; £8 × 7 = **£56**. More than half of £96; other share £40.
- T2: 18 ÷ 2 = 9; 9 × 7 = **63 litres of water**. Between 3 and 4 times the juice amount (54–72 litres).
- T3: 8 − 5 = 3; 21 ÷ 3 = 7; (5 + 8) × 7 = **91 cm**. The two lengths are 35 cm and 56 cm; difference 21 cm.

## Checks and review points

- 1 full worked demo, 2 step-by-step practice slides and 4 complete independent grids; 72 distinct independent questions.
- All count questions give whole numbers. In each generated share, the amounts sum to the stated total and satisfy the ratio; difference questions match the stated difference.
- Core alternates which amount is given and includes finding the total. Depth includes either group being larger and asks for a named amount or the total.
- Main point for review: are sharing a total / given one amount / given a difference the right three challenge threads for this group?
- A separate ratio-simplification module has not been added to this draft. This module assumes students can read ratio notation and count parts.
- The longer question cells need a readability check when this agreed content is laid out; do not solve a fit problem by dropping questions or shrinking text excessively.
