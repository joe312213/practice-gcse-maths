# M06 — Percentages of amounts and percentage change

**Status: Draft — awaiting review. Not approved for PowerPoint.**

## Scope

Distinguish the percentage amount, the new amount after a change, and the percentage change relative to the original amount. Reverse percentages are a separate module.

Challenge progression: find a percentage amount → apply an increase/decrease → calculate percentage change.

Calculator proposal: no calculator for the chosen values.

## Slide 1 — Worked demo

Instruction: **Follow each step down your column.**

| Row | Start | Build | Confidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The Full Question | A £80 ticket is discounted by 25%. What is the discount? | A £80 ticket is discounted by 25%. What is the new price? | A price rises from £80 to £100. Find the percentage increase. |
| Keywords & Calculation | Find 25% of £80. | Find the discount, then the remaining price. | Change relative to the original £80. |
| Written Method | £80 ÷ 4 = £20. | 25% of £80 = £20; £80 − £20 = £60. | £100 − £80 = £20; 20 ÷ 80 × 100 = 25%. |
| Ballpark Check & Math | 25% is a quarter; four lots of £20 make £80. | 75% remains; £60 is three quarters of £80. | A quarter of £80 is £20; £80 + £20 = £100. |
| Final Answer | £20 discount. | £60. | 25% increase. |

### Diagram / layout specification

Use an editable bar labelled £80 / 100%, split into four equal sections. Mark the £20 discount as one section, the £60 new price as three. For Depth show £80 as 100%, with a £20 extension labelled 25%.

Teacher working:

- T1: 150÷5 = **£30**.
- T2: 20% = £30; 150+30 = **£180**.
- T3: (180−150)÷150×100 = **20%**.

## Checks and review points

- Always divide the change by the original amount when finding percentage change.
- Do not confuse the discount amount with the final price.
- A decrease is reported as a positive percentage decrease, not an unexplained negative number.

- Seven slides; 72 distinct independent questions, plus the worked demo and two practice grids.
- Keep these exact values when creating slides. Recheck readability before publishing.
- The proposed challenge progression, calculator policy and row wording require user review.
