# M11 — Forming equations from problems

**Status: Draft — awaiting review. Not approved for PowerPoint.**

M10 starts with an equation already written. This module starts with words or a diagram: students form the equation, then solve and interpret it. The title names the distinguishing skill; solving remains the final step.

## Scope

Translate word problems into an equation, define the unknown, solve, and interpret the solution. Always ask students to form an equation, even when they could calculate mentally.

Challenge progression: one relationship → repeated cost plus a fixed charge → perimeter or equal-cost relationships.

Calculator proposal: no calculator.

## Row labels and practice prompts

Use these exact labels and prompts on both step-by-step practice slides. Repeat the same prompt across columns unless a genuine difference in the task requires a change.

| Row label | Student prompt |
| --- | --- |
| Keywords & Calculation | Choose a letter for the unknown. Write an equation from the question. |
| Written Method | Solve your equation using a written method. |
| Ballpark Check & Math | Check your solution against the information in the question. |
| Final Answer | Answer the original question. Include units where needed. |

## Slide 1 — Worked demo

Instruction: **Follow each step down your column.**

| Row | Thread 1: Guided | Thread 2: Core | Thread 3: Depth |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The Full Question | I add 7 to a number and get 19. Find the number. | Three identical tickets and a £5 booking fee cost £26. Find the price of one ticket. | A rectangle is 3 cm longer than it is wide. Its perimeter is 34 cm. Find its width. |
| Keywords & Calculation | Let x be the number. x + 7 = 19. | Let x be one ticket price in pounds. 3x + 5 = 26. | Let w be width in cm. Length is w+3. 2w + 2(w+3) = 34. |
| Written Method | Subtract 7: x = 12. | 3x=21; x=7. | 4w+6=34; 4w=28; w=7. |
| Ballpark Check & Math | 12 + 7 = 19. | 3×£7+£5=£26. | Length 10 cm; 2×7+2×10=34 cm. |
| Final Answer | The number is 12. | £7 per ticket. | Width 7 cm. |

### Diagram / layout specification

For Depth draw an editable rectangle with width w cm and length (w+3) cm. Put “Perimeter = 34 cm” beside it and mark “Not to scale”. Do not label the unknown sides with their answers until the working has found them.

| I subtract 8 from a number and get 15. Find the number. | Five identical tickets and a £7 booking fee cost £42. Find one ticket price. | A rectangle is 4 cm longer than it is wide. Its perimeter is 40 cm. Find its width. |

Teacher working:

- T1: x−8=15; **x=23**. Check 23−8=15.
- T2: 5x+7=42; 5x=35; **£7**.
- T3: 2w+2(w+4)=40; 4w=32; **8 cm**. Length12; perimeter40.

## Checks and review points

- Student instruction for this whole module: **Form an equation, solve it and check your answer.** Add this to each independent slide, alongside the standard instruction.
- Defining the unknown and forming the equation are part of the answer, not optional teacher working.
- Perimeter includes both lengths and both widths. Equal-cost questions compare total costs, not just per-visit charges.
- Review the proposed adapted prompts: labels remain the established application labels.

- Seven slides; 72 distinct independent questions, plus the worked demo and two practice grids.
- Keep these exact values when creating slides. Recheck readability before publishing.
- The proposed challenge progression, calculator policy and row wording require user review.
