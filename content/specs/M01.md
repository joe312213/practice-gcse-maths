# M01 — Lattice multiplication: website specification

Current website behaviour; historical authored teaching sources and question IDs are preserved. See the [teaching index](../../docs/INDEX.md#teaching-content) for provenance and source material.

## Question wording

Default `questionPrefix`: **Work out the product**. Show it immediately above the question in the answering area. Contextual applications override this with **Solve the problem**. Keep the input label **Answer**; provide required units separately.

## Challenge and variety

Confidence method questions, including spot-the-error investigations, must use 4×3, 3×4 or 4×4 digit multiplication. Include 4×4 questions on every Confidence error page; the current set has two per page. Most cell products should have two digits and the correct working should require several diagonal carries. A five-digit factor padded with zeros multiplied by one digit is not suitable Confidence lattice work. Vary both operands and fault positions rather than repeating one multiplier with near-identical first factors. Simple illustrations in the misconception table explain the error; they are not a licence to lower the live question's challenge.

## Sensible quick checks

No calculator needed. Round each factor to one significant figure and multiply the rounded values mentally; compare the scale with the exact answer. For example, 4,312 × 156 is about 4,000 × 200 = 800,000, so 672,672 has a plausible size. This is a ballpark check, not proof or a fixed percentage tolerance. Check the last digit separately where helpful. Do not require the learner to divide a large product to check a non-calculator method.


## Common errors

Use the [spot-error authoring process](../../docs/SPOT_ERRORS_PROCESS.md). The seven error families below reflect the user’s agreed simplification on 16 October 2026: reversed cell digits belong to the wrong-product family, not a separate diagnosis. The all-topic draft pass is authorised; live-bank migration follows content and UI review. IDs describe misconception coverage, not new question IDs.

### Agreed prototype coverage

| ID | Error | Illustrative mistake and intended repair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| L1 | The product is wrong | Include incorrect multiplication (4 × 6 shown as 14 instead of 24) and reversed digits (3 × 4 shown as 21 instead of 12, or 3 × 2 shown as 60 instead of 6). Use one option: “The product is wrong.” Propagate the displayed product through the totals; correction feedback gives the correct product and its tens/units. |
| L2 | Carry omitted from the next diagonal | In 89 × 7, the units diagonal totals 13 but its carry is omitted from the tens diagonal. Include that carry to obtain 623. |
| L3 | Right-hand factor digits written bottom-up | Lay out 34 × 16 as 34 × 61, obtaining 2074 instead of 544. Repair the factor order; the resulting cells are consequences. |
| L4 | Oversized carry read as an extra answer digit | In 76 × 7, correctly calculate the diagonal totals but copy the full-size carry into the answer, giving 5132 instead of 532. Distinguish carry notation from output digits. |

### Additional agreed coverage — 16 October 2026

| ID | Error | Illustrative mistake and intended repair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| L6 | Adding a digit along the wrong diagonal | In 23 × 4, include the units 2 from the 12 cell in the tens diagonal instead of the units diagonal, producing 110 rather than 92. Add each triangular entry exactly once in its own diagonal. |
| L7 | Adding a carry twice | In 76 × 7, the tens diagonal totals 13 and carries 1. Add that carry twice to the hundreds diagonal: 4 + 1 + 1 = 6, producing 632 instead of 532. Include it once: 4 + 1 = 5. |
| L8 | Dropping an internal zero when reading the answer | The lattice for 101 × 2 correctly gives hundreds 2, tens 0, units 2, but the answer is copied as 22. Retain the zero to give 202. A variant may insert an extra internal zero despite correct totals. |

Select the faulty cell, diagonal/carry region or answer read-off position, including correct and empty positions. L5 is merged into L1; retain the other family IDs. Do not offer competing fact/placement diagnoses or display a hidden correct calculation beside an incorrect cell. Working details must describe the product actually shown. L6 needs a visible addition line or trace showing which entries were used; a wrong total alone does not establish the cause. L7 must show the carry counted twice, rather than merely a wrong sum. L8 needs correct diagonal totals and a visibly incorrect read-off; include a selectable answer region when implementing this family. Writing a leading zero in a cell is useful but leaving the tens half blank is not an error when it still represents zero unambiguously. Dropping a leading zero from the final answer is also valid. Do not count these valid notational alternatives as L1/L8 mistakes. Simple illustrations isolate each error; authored questions must retain the agreed level of challenge and independent-error checks.


## Authorised interactive prototype — 15 October 2026

Follow the revised [spot-error process](../../docs/SPOT_ERRORS_PROCESS.md). The agreed first prototype covers a wrong cell product, a missed carry and their combination as two independent mistakes. Cells and diagonal-total/carry regions are selectable whether correct or incorrect. Later incorrect totals caused by a cell mistake must not require extra selections. The remaining families above are now agreed for drafting; their diagram renderers still require implementation/review. Prototype examples are separately authored in [lattice_error_prototype.json](../lattice_error_prototype.json); they do not replace live IDs or attempts.

Follow-up approval: add right-hand digits written bottom-up and a carry written at answer-digit size then copied as an extra answer digit. The prototype now includes 34 × 16 laid out as 34 × 61 (2074 instead of 544), and 76 × 7 with the hundreds carry included as an extra digit (5132 instead of 532). The latter keeps diagonal addition correct: this is a notation/read-off mistake, not double-counting a carry in addition. The whole right-hand factor is selectable in every example, including correct setups.
