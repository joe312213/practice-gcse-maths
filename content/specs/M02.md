# M02 — Bus stop division: website specification

Current website behaviour; historical authored teaching sources and question IDs are preserved. See the [teaching index](../../docs/INDEX.md#teaching-content) for provenance and source material.

## Question wording

Default `questionPrefix`: **Work out the division**. Show it immediately above the question in the answering area. Contextual applications override this with **Solve the problem**. Keep the input label **Answer**; provide required units separately.

## Sensible quick checks

No calculator needed. Estimate using nearby easy multiples of the divisor, and check the quotient has a sensible place value. For a remainder answer, it must be non-negative and smaller than the divisor. When manageable mentally, multiply the quotient by the divisor and add the remainder (145 ÷ 6 gives 24 r 1: 24 × 6 + 1 = 145). Use an estimate instead of a burdensome inverse calculation for long decimal answers.


## Common errors — agreed 16 October 2026

Agreed by the user on 16 October 2026 for the [spot-error authoring process](../../docs/SPOT_ERRORS_PROCESS.md). These define accepted misconception coverage with illustrative mistakes; agreement does not itself replace live bank questions.

| ID | Misconception / error | Illustrative mistake and intended repair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| D1 | Choosing an incorrect quotient digit from a division/multiplication fact | In 43 ÷ 8, choosing 6 groups rather than 5. Use 8 × 5 = 40; 8 × 6 exceeds 43. A digit that is too small can instead leave a remainder at least as large as the divisor. |
| D2 | Calculating the remainder incorrectly despite choosing the correct quotient digit | Five groups of 8 from 43, but writing 43 − 40 = 2. The remainder is 3. |
| D3 | Losing or transferring a remainder with the wrong place value | In 178 ÷ 4, after 17 ÷ 4 = 4 r 1, the next amount is 18, not 1 + 8 = 9 or just 8. |
| D4 | Omitting a necessary zero in the quotient / misaligning quotient digits | Writing 816 ÷ 4 = 24 instead of 204. Record the zero tens before proceeding to the units. |
| D5 | Discarding a final remainder when a remainder answer is required | Writing 178 ÷ 4 = 44 rather than 44 r 2. A remainder must be included and smaller than the divisor. |
| D6 | Treating a remainder as a decimal digit instead of continuing the division | Writing 5 ÷ 4 = 1.1 because the remainder is 1. Continue with 10 tenths, then 20 hundredths, to obtain 1.25. |
| D7 | Misplacing the decimal point when continuing to a decimal answer | Writing 45 ÷ 6 = 75 rather than 7.5. Place the quotient decimal over the dividend decimal and continue with zeros. |

Select quotient positions (including omitted-zero positions), remainder transfers or decimal placement. Distinguish D1 from D2 by showing the intermediate multiplication/subtraction clearly. A wrong remainder caused by a wrong quotient digit is not a second independent error. D5 applies to questions asking for a remainder; do not mark a mathematically equivalent decimal wrong when the question permits it. D6/D7 apply only where decimal continuation is taught/requested. Start with one independent mistake; combine mistakes only when each remains separately identifiable. Retain current level boundaries rather than adding harder arithmetic just to manufacture errors.

## Spot-error level and variety review — 16 October 2026

Start uses divisions whose correct working needs no remainder transfers. Build uses varied single-digit divisors, carried remainders and a nonzero final remainder. Confidence uses four-digit dividends and two-digit divisors, with terminating decimal continuation. Across twelve questions use at least five distinct Build divisors and six Confidence divisors. Show decimal continuation in corrected feedback.
