## Challenge and pages

- **Start:** select multiplication or exact division for equal groups, sharing, length and money. An occasional explicit metre-to-centimetre error develops unit checking.
- **Build:** distinguish full packs, enough vehicles/containers, leftover material and exact money shares; vary objectives so division does not always mean rounding the same way. Rate/area questions combine two given quantities before multiplying; avoid single-product questions indistinguishable from Start. The Build card/demo combines pupils and adults, divides by passenger seats, then rounds up to enough minibuses.
- **Confidence:** link two operations, including interpreting a remainder before a cost calculation, an extra charge/discount, stock used, repeated groups and sharing a total cost. Challenge comes from decisions as well as operands.

## Method animation

Follow the [shared contract](../../docs/METHOD_ANIMATIONS.md). Inspect the chosen operands, then reveal their result. For remainder problems, show the direct division first (for example, 130 ÷ 6 = 21 r 4); next highlight the decision to retain the quotient, take the remainder or add one group. Keep total = full groups × group size + remainder as an inverse check, rather than the main working. Reveal that interpreted result before using it in the next calculation. Never show a cost based on unrounded group requirements. Completed working stays visible; pause/seek/replay reproduce the same input/result phases. Finish with the inverse/context check and units. A word problem need not have an ornamental diagram; its explicit, highlighted calculation sequence is the teaching visual.

## Sensible quick checks

No calculator needed. Estimate using friendly numbers before interpreting the result in context. Whole packs, journeys and containers must use the appropriate whole-number direction: 130 biscuits in packs of 6 makes 21 full packs, but carrying 130 items in containers holding 6 needs 22 containers. Check units and whether a remainder is used, discarded or rounded up.


## Common errors — agreed 16 October 2026

Agreed by the user on 16 October 2026 for the [spot-error authoring process](../../docs/SPOT_ERRORS_PROCESS.md). These define accepted misconception coverage with illustrative mistakes; agreement does not itself replace live bank questions.

| ID | Misconception / error | Illustrative mistake and intended repair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A1 | Choosing addition where equal groups require multiplication, or multiplication where sharing/grouping requires division | Eight boxes of six items are modelled as 8 + 6 = 14. Use eight groups of six: 48. Accept correct repeated addition as a valid method. |
| A2 | Reversing the division operands | For 48 biscuits packed six per bag, use 6 ÷ 48. Divide the total by the bag size: 48 ÷ 6 = 8 bags. |
| A3 | Omitting a required quantity before calculating groups | For 46 pupils and 5 adults in minibuses with 10 passenger seats, divide only 46 by 10 and give 5 buses. Include all 51 passengers, requiring 6 buses. |
| A4 | Rounding automatically rather than interpreting the required whole groups | 13 people in six-seat vehicles require 3 vehicles, but 13 biscuits make only 2 full packs of six. Rounding down in the first case or up in the second is wrong. Use separate examples with explicit objectives. |
| A5 | Giving the quotient when the remainder is requested, or attaching the wrong meaning to the remainder | From 17 m of ribbon cut into 3 m lengths, 17 ÷ 3 = 5 r 2. Giving “5 m left” confuses the five full lengths with the 2 m left. |
| A6 | Using the wrong intermediate quantity in a later calculation | Correctly find 3 vehicles for 13 people, then price 13 vehicles at £20 each. Use the calculated 3 vehicles: £60. |
| A7 | Repeating a one-off charge or omitting a required final step | Four £6 tickets plus one £3 booking fee are calculated as 4 × (6 + 3) = £36. The fee applies once: 4 × 6 + 3 = £27. Stopping at £24 is an alternative example of the omitted-fee fault. |
| A8 | Combining incompatible units without conversion | Cut 2 m of ribbon into 25 cm lengths using 2 ÷ 25. Convert first: 200 ÷ 25 = 8 lengths. |

Select the chosen calculation, combined quantity, interpretation/rounding decision or linked step. Wording must make whole packs, enough capacity, leftover material and one-off versus per-item charges explicit. A6 must show the correct earlier result; if the wrong count follows an earlier rounding error it is consequential, not another independent fault. Do not assume every division remainder requires rounding up, or that repeated addition is inherently wrong. Units belong in the displayed working; the app still accepts the numeric answer with its unit label. Where written arithmetic is displayed, reuse the agreed lattice/division error families rather than inventing a second algorithm specification here. Retain accessible arithmetic and the current Start/Build/Confidence progression.
