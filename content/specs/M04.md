# M04 — Ratios: sharing and missing amounts: website

Implemented 12 October 2026; Draft status removed at the user’s request on 13 October 2026. The [historical teaching source](../M04_ratio.md) is preserved, including its [audit](../M04_ratio_audit.md). Website extensions use stable `WEB` IDs and source provenance. No slide regeneration.

No calculator needed. Distinguish total, known named amount and difference; find one equal part and answer the requested quantity. Start uses friendly two-part ratios; Build and Confidence also compare amounts and find differences. Never request the same quantity already supplied. Contexts mix money, mixtures, quantities, time and counts. Do not tie a context to one kind of given. Equal boxes label both bars by name; every box has the same value. Confidence practice links ratio reasoning to a further calculation in two thirds of ordinary questions: 24/36 independent, 8/12 scaffolded and 8/12 error questions. Retain the remaining third for consolidating core ratio reasoning. All 18 Confidence problem-stage questions link calculations. Use realistic prices, repeated recipe batches, extra rest, quantities used, centimetres-to-metres conversion and simple percentages of money shares; stay within four arithmetic operations, including the ratio-parts sum/difference. Higher demand comes from linking quantities and choosing operations, not needlessly large numbers.

Confidence Learn the method uses a worked cost example (ratio 3:7, a 28-litre difference, white paint at £3/litre → £147) and a conversion recap (75 cm for five parts, nine parts → 1.35 m). Its guidance explicitly covers the follow-on operation. Scaffolded questions include authored steps and units for the further calculation; the Confidence problem-help example links a money share to saving 25%. Keep guidance, hints, answers, checks and error repairs aligned.

Build also has a light introduction to linked problems: 9/36 independent, 3/12 scaffolded and 3/12 error questions, plus the recap. These start with one known ratio part, so only two actual calculations are needed: multiply to find the other quantity, then find its cost, subtract beads used or add ribbon. Keep the main Build demonstration focused on simplification.

Selected Build questions and the worked example explicitly simplify ratios: divide **both** parts by the same common factor, then use the simplified parts consistently for the known quantity, one-part calculation, requested amount and diagram. For example, 6:10 becomes 3:5 by dividing both parts by 2; sharing £160 then uses eight parts, with £100 for the five-part share. Do not change only one part or mix original and simplified part values.

Each bank contains 243 questions plus three recaps: three pages per level of 12 independent, four scaffolded, four error and six problem questions, one demo and two assessment candidates per level. Assessments draw two per level. Each problem has level-matched help, units, an independent calculation model, a check and specific misconception feedback. Error correction choices describe repairs without revealing the final answer.

Shared playback handles all examples and diagrams; calculators are explicitly allowed for M07/M12, without implying these topics occur only on calculator papers. No exam board is assumed. ## Question wording

Default `questionPrefix`: **Solve the ratio problem**. Show it immediately above the question in the answering area. Contextual applications override this with **Solve the problem**. Keep the input label **Answer**; provide required units separately.

## Sensible quick checks

No calculator needed. Add shares back to the stated total, or compare their difference with the given difference. Divide each named share by its ratio part where mental arithmetic is straightforward: both should give the same value of one part. For £40 shared 2:3, £16 + £24 = £40 and 16 ÷ 2 = 24 ÷ 3 = 8. Check that the larger number of parts gets the larger amount.

## Units in displayed working

Label intermediate measured amounts and final answers with their appropriate units; prices use a leading £ (after any negative sign). Keep ratio parts and percentage proportions dimensionless. Students enter only the numeric part in the app; Learn the method reminds them to include units in exam working and answers.
