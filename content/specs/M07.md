# M07 — Reverse percentages: website

Implemented 12 October 2026; Draft status removed at the user’s request on 13 October 2026. The [historical teaching source](../M07_reverse_percentages.md) is preserved. Website extensions use stable `WEB` IDs and source provenance. No slide regeneration.

Calculator allowed. Prerequisites: percentages of amounts and percentage change (M06). Start recovers a whole from a known percentage; Build reverses increases and reductions; Confidence removes a separate fixed addition after the percentage change before recovering the original and may scale to multiple equal amounts. Delivery is one example alongside non-financial contexts. Teach the original as 100%, identify the known percentage, use its decimal multiplier and divide. The bar diagram separates delivery from the percentage amount. Opposite-percentage multiplication of the changed amount is a misconception, not a valid inverse.

Each bank contains 243 questions plus three recaps: three pages per level of 12 independent, four scaffolded, four error and six problem questions, one demo and two assessment candidates per level. Assessments draw two per level. Each problem has level-matched help, units, an independent calculation model, a check and specific misconception feedback. Error correction choices describe repairs without revealing the final answer.

Shared playback handles all examples and diagrams; calculators are explicitly allowed for M07/M12, without implying these topics occur only on calculator papers. No exam board is assumed. Common scope follows the [DfE subject content](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/gcse-mathematics-subject-content-and-assessment-objectives). ## Question wording

Default `questionPrefix`: **Find the original amount**. Show it immediately above the question in the answering area. Contextual applications override this with **Solve the problem**. Keep the input label **Answer**; provide required units separately.

## Sensible quick checks

Calculator allowed. Apply the forward multiplier to the proposed original value and compare with the given final amount. If a 20% reduction leaves £64, £80 × 0.8 = £64. Also check direction: before a reduction the original must be larger, and before an increase it must be smaller. Keep full calculator precision until the required final rounding.

## Units in displayed working

Label intermediate measured amounts and final answers with their appropriate units; prices use a leading £ (after any negative sign). Keep ratio parts and percentage proportions dimensionless. Students enter only the numeric part in the app; Learn the method reminds them to include units in exam working and answers.


## Common errors — agreed 16 October 2026

Agreed by the user on 16 October 2026 for the [spot-error authoring process](../../docs/SPOT_ERRORS_PROCESS.md). These define accepted misconception coverage with illustrative mistakes; agreement does not itself replace live bank questions.

| ID | Misconception / error | Illustrative mistake and intended repair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| RP1 | Assigning 100% to an amount known to be only part of the original | £48 is 60% of the original, but label £48 as 100% and retain it as the whole. The original is 48 ÷ 0.6 = £80. |
| RP2 | Using the percentage change instead of the percentage represented by the final amount | After a 20% reduction the price is £64; calculate 64 ÷ 0.2. £64 represents 80%, so divide by 0.8 to get £80. After an increase, use 100% plus the increase. |
| RP3 | Multiplying by the forward multiplier instead of dividing | Undo a 20% reduction by calculating 64 × 0.8 = £51.20. Divide by 0.8 to recover £80. |
| RP4 | Trying to undo a change with the opposite percentage of the changed amount | Undo a 20% reduction by adding 20% to £64, obtaining £76.80. The percentage is now based on a different whole; use 64 ÷ 0.8 = £80. |
| RP5 | Converting the known percentage/multiplier with the wrong place value | After a 25% increase, correctly identify 125% but convert it to 0.125 rather than 1.25. For a final £100, divide by 1.25 to recover £80. |
| RP6 | Failing to remove a separate fixed addition before reversing the percentage | A total of £69 includes £5 delivery added after a 20% reduction; use 69 ÷ 0.8 − 5 = £81.25. Remove delivery first: (69 − 5) ÷ 0.8 = £80. |
| RP7 | Stopping at the recovered unit amount or scaling the wrong amount | One item costs £64 after a 20% reduction. Correctly recover £80 each but give £80 when asked for the original cost of three items. Complete 3 × 80 = £240. |

Select the percentage label, multiplier conversion, inverse operation, fee-removal step or final scaling. Accept finding 1% then 100%, a correct multiplier division or an equation. RP1–RP4 describe distinct possible starting faults, but do not count a wrong multiplier/answer that simply follows an earlier misidentified percentage as an extra independent error. Fee questions must state when the fee was applied and whether it is per order or per item; do not assume every fixed amount belongs outside the percentage change. Keep calculators allowed, retain precision until the requested final rounding, and preserve Start/Build/Confidence scope. Forward checking must use the original operation and reinstate any removed fee.

## Spot-error level and variety review — 16 October 2026

Reverse percentages need not be financial: include volume, mass, membership or length. Confidence uses a separate fixed addition after the percentage change (delivery, packaging, top-up or visitors), removed before reversing; some questions then scale the original amount. State the order and what the requested original includes. Across twelve Confidence investigations include at least four non-financial examples, four increases and four reductions. Keep counts whole. Multiplying by the forward multiplier (RP3) differs from applying the opposite percentage (RP4).

The read-only live-bank audit enforces measurable requirements. Review wording, realism and causal working using the [authoring process](../../docs/SPOT_ERRORS_PROCESS.md).
