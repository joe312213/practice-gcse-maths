# M12 — Speed, distance and time: website

Implemented 12 October 2026; Draft status removed at the user’s request on 13 October 2026. The [historical teaching source](../M12_speed_distance_and_time.md) is preserved. Website extensions use stable `WEB` IDs and source provenance. No slide regeneration.

Calculator allowed. Start varies the requested speed, distance or time with matching hours/km units. Build includes conversion of minutes to hours; Confidence uses total distance and total elapsed time across two journey parts, including a stop where stated. Retain calculator precision until the final rounding to two decimal places. Never average two speeds without weighting time; show approximate equality for a rounded result. Journey diagrams label the given quantities and any stop. Question numbers use plausible cyclist/vehicle/ferry speeds.

Each bank contains 243 questions plus three recaps: three pages per level of 12 independent, four scaffolded, four error and six problem questions, one demo and two assessment candidates per level. Assessments draw two per level. Each problem has level-matched help, units, an independent calculation model, a check and specific misconception feedback. Error correction choices describe repairs without revealing the final answer.

Shared playback handles all examples and diagrams; calculators are explicitly allowed for M07/M12, without implying these topics occur only on calculator papers. No exam board is assumed. Common scope follows the [DfE subject content](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/gcse-mathematics-subject-content-and-assessment-objectives). See [problem-solving behaviour](PROBLEM_SOLVING.md).

## Question wording

Default `questionPrefix`: **Solve the speed, distance or time problem**. Show it immediately above the question in the answering area. Contextual applications override this with **Solve the problem**. Keep the input label **Answer**; provide required units separately.

## Sensible quick checks

Calculator allowed. Substitute the result into distance = speed × time with matching units, and compare with the original data. For 150 km in 2.5 hours, 60 km/h × 2.5 h = 150 km. Estimate travel distance in one hour as a reasonableness check. Convert minutes to hours by dividing by 60, not by 100; check rounding only at the end.

## Units in displayed working

Label intermediate measured amounts and final answers with their appropriate units; prices use a leading £ (after any negative sign). Keep ratio parts and percentage proportions dimensionless. Students enter only the numeric part in the app; Learn the method reminds them to include units in exam working and answers.
