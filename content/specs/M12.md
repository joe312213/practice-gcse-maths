## Question wording

Default `questionPrefix`: **Solve the speed, distance or time problem**. Show it immediately above the question in the answering area. Contextual applications override this with **Solve the problem**. Keep the input label **Answer**; provide required units separately.

## Sensible quick checks

Calculator allowed. Substitute the result into distance = speed × time with matching units, and compare with the original data. For 150 km in 2.5 hours, 60 km/h × 2.5 h = 150 km. Estimate travel distance in one hour as a reasonableness check. Convert minutes to hours by dividing by 60, not by 100; check rounding only at the end.

## Units in displayed working

Label intermediate measured amounts and final answers with their appropriate units; prices use a leading £ (after any negative sign). Keep ratio parts and percentage proportions dimensionless. Students enter only the numeric part in the app; Learn the method reminds them to include units in exam working and answers.


## Common errors — agreed 16 October 2026

Agreed by the user on 16 October 2026 for the [spot-error authoring process](../../docs/SPOT_ERRORS_PROCESS.md). These define accepted misconception coverage with illustrative mistakes; agreement does not itself replace live bank questions.

| ID | Misconception / error | Illustrative mistake and intended repair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| T1 | Choosing the wrong relationship or reversing a division | For 120 km in 2 hours, calculate speed as 120 × 2 = 240. Use distance ÷ time = 60 km/h. For time, use distance ÷ speed, not the reverse. |
| T2 | Treating minutes as hundredths of an hour | 90 minutes → 0.9 hours. Divide by 60: 1.5 hours. |
| T3 | Reading decimal hours as clock minutes | Read 1.5 hours as 1 hour 50 minutes (or 1 hour 5 minutes). The fractional hour is 0.5 × 60 = 30 minutes. |
| T4 | Calculating with one unit but labelling the result with another | 120 km ÷ 90 minutes = 1.33… km/min, labelled as km/h. Convert the rate by multiplying by 60, or divide by 1.5 hours, to obtain 80 km/h. |
| T5 | Combining the whole journey's distance with only part of its time, or vice versa | Travel 30 km in 1 hour, then 120 km in 2 hours; calculate 150 ÷ 2 = 75 km/h. The matching totals are 150 km and 3 hours, giving 50 km/h. |
| T6 | Omitting a stop when the requested average includes it | Travel 120 km in 2 moving hours with an additional 30-minute stop; give 60 km/h for the whole journey including the stop. Use 120 ÷ 2.5 = 48 km/h. |
| T7 | Taking an unweighted mean of speeds when time at each speed differs | One hour at 30 km/h and two hours at 60 km/h: (30 + 60) ÷ 2 = 45. Total distance ÷ total time gives 150 ÷ 3 = 50 km/h. |
| T8 | Rounding an intermediate conversion/calculation too early | Convert 20 minutes to 0.33 hours, then calculate 10 ÷ 0.33 = 30.30 km/h. Retain 20/60 hours to obtain 30 km/h, then round only the requested final result. |

Select a relationship, unit conversion, journey-total line or final interpretation. Make the requested unit and treatment of stops explicit. Working consistently in km/min before conversion is valid; T4 is the wrong label or missing required conversion. An ordinary mean of two speeds is valid when equal times are spent at each speed, so T7 examples must use unequal durations and unequal speeds. Moving-time speed is valid when requested; do not force stops into that calculation. T2/T3 are conversion-direction variants, not automatically independent errors in one example. T8 must use quantities for which early rounding actually changes the required final answer. Keep calculators allowed and retain precision until final rounding.

## Spot-error level and variety review — 16 October 2026

Use realistic subjects and quantities: ordinary cyclists should not travel at motorway speeds. Write journey measurements as familiar decimals rather than awkward improper fractions. Build varies minute conversions and fractional hours. Confidence varies two-leg journeys, stops and unequal-duration speed calculations. For T7, different speeds must apply for different durations so their unweighted mean is actually wrong. Use at least four stop durations across the Confidence set.

The read-only live-bank audit enforces measurable requirements. Review wording, realism and causal working using the [authoring process](../../docs/SPOT_ERRORS_PROCESS.md).
