## Challenge progression

- Start: add/subtract positive integers, including crossing zero and answers of either sign.
- Build: add/subtract negative integers. Distinguish the operation from the sign attached to the number.
- Confidence: two successive signed operations, retaining an intermediate result.

Use no-calculator integers. Subtraction adds the opposite while keeping the first number unchanged. Never apply “two minuses make a plus” as a universal rule. Diagnose reversed direction, discarded signs and counting the starting tick instead of intervals.

## Method animation

For each operation: inspect the current value, operation and signed operand; reveal the signed operation conversion (for example + (−3) = −3); inspect the original calculation and its simplified equivalent (6 + (−3) = 6 − 3); then reveal the result and number-line move. Use that result as the next input. Keep completed arrows/results visible, equal tick spacing, labelled zero/start/end and clear arrowheads. Later arrows must not appear early. End with an inverse check.

## Question wording

## Sensible quick checks

No calculator needed. Predict direction before calculating: adding a positive or subtracting a negative moves right; adding a negative or subtracting a positive moves left. Check that the answer lies on the expected side of the starting number, using a short mental number-line move. For example, 6 + (−3) must be less than 6 and gives 3. For multiple changes, combine the net change when easy.


## Common errors — agreed 16 October 2026

Agreed by the user on 16 October 2026 for the [spot-error authoring process](../../docs/SPOT_ERRORS_PROCESS.md). These define accepted misconception coverage with illustrative mistakes; agreement does not itself replace live bank questions.

| ID | Misconception / error | Illustrative mistake and intended repair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| S1 | Losing the sign of the starting number | Rewriting −3 + 5 as 3 + 5. Keep the starting value −3; the answer is 2. |
| S2 | Treating addition of a negative as addition of a positive | Rewriting 6 + (−3) as 6 + 3. It is 6 − 3 = 3. |
| S3 | Failing to reverse the change when subtracting a negative | Rewriting 6 − (−3) as 6 − 3. Subtracting −3 adds 3, giving 9. |
| S4 | Applying “two minuses make a plus” to unrelated signs | Rewriting −4 − 3 as 4 + 3. The starting sign and subtraction operation do not cancel; the result is −7. |
| S5 | Choosing the wrong sign after calculating the difference of magnitudes | Writing −7 + 3 = 4. The result remains below zero: −4. |
| S6 | Number-line direction or counting error despite a correct symbolic operation | Moving left for −2 + 5, or counting the starting tick as move one and stopping at 2. Move five intervals right to 3. These are separate candidate faults, not automatically two errors in one diagram. |
| S7 | Starting a later operation from zero/original input instead of the intermediate result | For −3 + 8 − 2, correctly reach 5, then restart the second arrow at 0. Continue from 5 to 3. |

Select the signed-operation rewrite, relevant number-line arrow/interval run, intermediate result or final calculation as appropriate. An arrow that follows an already incorrect sign conversion is consequential; assess the conversion, not both. Keep Start to positive operands/crossing zero, Build to adding/subtracting negatives, and Confidence to successive operations. Do not introduce signed multiplication/division or broader order-of-operations content from M14.

## Spot-error level and variety review — 16 October 2026

Confidence investigations use two operations, with at least six different intermediate results across twelve questions and intermediate values on both sides of zero. Vary signs, directions and zero crossing; do not repeat one intermediate value with cosmetic operand changes. Propagate a mistaken first move without adding accidental later sign faults.
