## Challenge progression

- Start: common denominators, both operations, simplification and results at/above one.
- Build: denominators where one is a multiple of the other; use equivalent fractions.
- Confidence: unrelated denominators (including shared factors) and mixed numbers.

Keep questions numerical and scaffolded subtraction non-negative. Model mixed-to-improper conversion before testing it. Diagrams must use equal-sized wholes. Diagnose combining denominators, changing the operation and simplifying only the numerator. Accept equivalent fractions, exact decimals and mixed numbers; simplification is taught but has no separate mark in this topic.

## Method animation

Inspect each mixed number then reveal its improper fraction. Highlight denominators, then reveal both equivalent fractions together, changing numerator and denominator consistently. Equal-whole strips show their common unit. Inspect the two numerators and operation, then reveal the new numerator over the unchanged denominator. Finally inspect that result and reveal its simplest equivalent form. Retain completed lines and end with the inverse operation using the original second operand.

## Sensible quick checks

No calculator needed. Compare with familiar benchmarks (0, one half and 1), and predict whether adding/subtracting a positive fraction must increase/decrease the first value. For 1/3 + 1/6, the answer should be between 1/3 and 2/3; 1/2 fits. Check equivalent fractions by multiplying numerator and denominator by the same factor and reduce only when both have a common factor. Do not demand decimal calculator conversion.


## Common errors — agreed 16 October 2026

Agreed by the user on 16 October 2026 for the [spot-error authoring process](../../docs/SPOT_ERRORS_PROCESS.md). These define accepted misconception coverage with illustrative mistakes; agreement does not itself replace live bank questions.

| ID | Misconception / error | Illustrative mistake and intended repair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| F1 | Adding/subtracting denominators as well as numerators | 3/8 + 2/8 = 5/16. The parts remain eighths: 5/8. |
| F2 | Changing a denominator without scaling the numerator by the same factor | Rewriting 1/3 as 1/6 rather than 2/6. Equivalent fractions must preserve the value. |
| F3 | Changing the requested operation or reversing subtraction | For 5/6 − 1/3, correctly rewrite 1/3 as 2/6, then add 5 + 2. Subtract 5 − 2, giving 3/6. Preserve the original order when subtracting. |
| F4 | Converting a mixed number to an improper fraction incorrectly | 2 1/3 → 3/3 rather than 7/3. Two wholes are six thirds, then add the remaining third. |
| F5 | Regrouping a whole as one fractional part | When subtracting 3/4 from 2 1/4, rewrite 2 1/4 as 1 2/4. One whole supplies four quarters: the correct regrouping is 1 5/4. |
| F6 | Simplifying only one part, or using different changes to numerator and denominator | 4/6 → 2/6. Divide both by 2 to obtain 2/3. |
| F7 | Cancelling across addition/subtraction as if multiplying | Cross-cancelling the two 2s in 2/3 + 1/2 to obtain 1/3 + 1. Use equivalent fractions over a common denominator; the sum is 7/6. |
| F8 | Converting an improper fraction back to a mixed number with the wrong fractional unit | 7/3 → 2 1/7. The remainder is still one third: 2 1/3. |

Select an equivalent-fraction conversion, numerator calculation, regrouping or simplification line. If fraction strips are shown, keep equal wholes and make both correct/incorrect locations selectable. Different valid common denominators and equivalent unsimplified answers are accepted; neither an unreduced answer nor a valid alternative method is a mistake. F3 is a distinct fault only when equivalence was handled correctly; otherwise a later wrong numerator may be consequential. Regrouping is an alternative to improper-fraction conversion, not a compulsory step. Restrict mixed-number faults to where the method has been modelled, and retain non-negative scaffolded subtraction. Do not introduce multiplication/division of fractions as new topic content through distractors.

## Spot-error level and variety review — 16 October 2026

Spot-error Build examples vary equivalence factors, including 2, 3 and 4 rather than always doubling. These requirements apply to the original calculation, independently of the inserted fault. Keep non-negative subtraction and exact equivalent answers.
