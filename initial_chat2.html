This is an excellent, highly effective pedagogical approach for GCSE Maths resit students.

Level 2 college resit students often struggle not with pure arithmetic, but with mathematical comprehension, anxiety, and knowing where to start on multi-step problems.

By decomposing the problem-solving process into a visual grid, you remove the "blank page" panic. It allows students to see that every complex problem is just a series of smaller, manageable tasks.

Here is a breakdown of how you can structure this framework, along with a concrete example of a slide grid.

🗺️ The Grid Framework

To make the slides scannable and consistent, keep the structure identical across different topics so students get used to the workflow.

Columns: The 3 Threads (Challenge Levels)

  1. Thread 1: Guided (Fluency & Structure) – For students who lack confidence. Highly scaffolded, often providing a starting diagram or partially filled steps.
  2. Thread 2: Core (Standard GCSE Foundation) – Mirrors a typical 3–4 mark GCSE exam question.
  3. Thread 3: Depth (Context & Problem Solving) – Same mathematical skill, but wrapped in a more complex real-world context or combined with a secondary topic (e.g., area combined with cost).

Rows: Decomposed Steps

  • Step 1: Decode & Represent – Highlighting key words, identifying the math topic, or drawing a visual aid (like a bar model).
  • Step 2: The Bridge (The Setup) – Setting up the calculation, forming an equation, or finding a single unit/part.
  • Step 3: Execute & Evaluate – Doing the final math and checking if the answer makes practical sense (e.g., "Can a person buy 4.3 tins of paint?").
